## Description

Found by P43's repo-wide grep for the four shapes it fixed (`waitUntilExit()` without `waitDraining`, `readToEnd()` after a wait, a `Pipe()` handed to a child and never read, a `readabilityHandler` never nilled), run over `scarf/scarf`, `scarf/Scarf iOS` and `scarf/Packages/ScarfCore/Sources`. P43 fixed everything in its own named scope; these are what the sweep found OUTSIDE it. All PRE-existing.

**0. (P46, the shape ProcessAsyncWaitP43cTests cannot see.)** The P43c sweep proves that three NAMED spellings (`waitUntilExit(timeout:`, `.waitDraining(`, `waitUntilExit()`) are not called directly from an `async` function body. It is blind to a blocking wait reached INDIRECTLY through a SYNCHRONOUS helper called from `async` code — and its calibration floor, being a count of what that matcher finds, blesses those by construction. P46 narrowed the suite's title and doc to what it proves and files the two live shapes here:

- `LocalTransport.runProcess` (`scarf/Packages/ScarfCore/Sources/ScarfCore/Transport/LocalTransport.swift:260`): synchronous, and its timeout arm is a `while proc.isRunning && Date() < deadline { Thread.sleep(forTimeInterval: 0.1) }` spin (`:317-320`). Called from `async` code through `Task.detached`, and from actor-isolated code that is not always detached.
- `SSHTransport.runLocal` (`.../Transport/SSHTransport.swift:1047`): synchronous, `DispatchGroup.wait(timeout:)` (`:1113-1116`) — bounded when a timeout is passed, UNBOUNDED in the `timeout == nil` arm below it.

Both are the "held cooperative thread" the P43c work exists to prevent, one indirection away. Fixing them means either making the transport's `runProcess` async end-to-end or auditing every call site for a detached hop; either way the P43c sweep should then be widened to follow one level of synchronous call, and its title widened back.

**1. `LocalTransport.runProcess` (`scarf/Packages/ScarfCore/Sources/ScarfCore/Transport/LocalTransport.swift:324`, `:331`).** Both pipes ARE drained concurrently by `ProcessPipeDrainer`, so there is no deadlock — but:
- `:324` is `terminate(); proc.waitUntilExit()` on the OVERRUN path. That is verbatim the anti-pattern `Process.waitDraining`'s doc comment calls out: a child that ignores SIGTERM, or is wedged in an uninterruptible kernel sleep, never reaps and the "bounded" call blocks forever after its budget is already spent. `waitUntilExit(timeout:)` already does SIGTERM → bounded poll → pid-guarded SIGKILL → bounded poll; use it.
- `:331` is the `timeout == nil` arm — a genuinely unbounded wait whenever a caller passes no timeout. Enumerate those callers and decide whether `timeout` should stop being optional.

**2. `SSHTransport` (`.../Transport/SSHTransport.swift:1122`, `:1133`).** The same two shapes: `:1122` is the bare wait after `terminate()` on the `waitGroup` timeout path, `:1133` the unbounded `else` arm. Same fix.

**3. The four streaming spawns read stderr AFTER the wait, with nothing draining it during the run** — `LocalTransport.swift:390` + `:393`, `:454` + `:457`; `SSHTransport.swift:782` + `:785`, `:854` + `:857`. Each drains STDOUT to EOF in a `while handle.availableData` loop, then `proc.waitUntilExit()` (unbounded), then `errPipe.readToEnd()` only when the exit is non-zero. A child with more than 64 KB of stderr blocks in `write()` while the parent is still pulling stdout, so neither side moves. `ssh -v` on a slow ProxyCommand and any Hermes verb that logs to stderr reach that size. Fix: drain both pipes concurrently (the `ProcessPipeDrainer` the non-streaming path already uses), and bound the wait.

**4. `TestConnectionProbe` (`scarf/scarf/Features/Servers/ViewModels/TestConnectionProbe.swift:182-202`).** Spawns `/usr/bin/ssh` with two pipes, neither drained during the run, and reads both with `readToEnd()` after a 20 s `Task.sleep` poll. The probe's whole point is a verbose ssh trace, so >64 KB of stderr is the EXPECTED case, not the corner: it wedges ssh, burns the full 20 s, and reports "Timed out after 20s" for a connection that was working. The poll itself is fine (it suspends rather than blocking the actor) — it is the undrained pipes that are the bug. Fix: `Process.waitDraining(timeout:pipes:)` (now in ScarfCore, `Models/ProcessTimeout.swift`).

**Verified NOT findings** (checked, leave alone): `ProcessACPChannel.swift:144`'s three `Pipe()`s are read by `PipeLineReader`; `SSHScriptRunner`'s `readabilityHandler`s are detached at `:267`; `OAuthFlowController:170` and `MCPLoginController:155` leave theirs installed deliberately, with the reason in the comment; `HermesProxyService`'s stderr handler is nilled in its termination handler.
